Proof of Concept

What investors need to see before they write a cheque

Before any investor — whether YC, a VC fund, or an angel — commits capital, they need evidence that your idea works, that people want it, and that the team can execute. Proof of Concept (POC) is that evidence package.

Proof of Concept (POC)

A demonstration — through a prototype, pilot, or early version — that your core technology or business model is viable in the real world. It answers: "Does this actually work?"

Lesedi Edge V.1 runs entirely on-device across 12 SA languages with zero data — a live, working POC that AI can be delivered offline in Africa.
Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

The simplest version of your product that delivers core value to early users and generates real feedback. An MVP is a POC that real users have tested.

Lesedi Edge V.1 Beta (launching August 2026) is UmkhoAI's MVP — a working offline AI that real South Africans can download and test before the nationwide launch.
Product-Market Fit (PMF)

The point at which your product satisfies a real market need so strongly that it grows organically. Investors at Seed and Series A look hard for early PMF signals.

600 million offline Africans represent a clearly defined, underserved market. UmkhoAI's offline-first architecture directly addresses their biggest barrier to AI access: data costs.

What YC and Top VCs Look For in POC

Signal 01

Working Technology

A demo or prototype the investor can see, touch, or use. Video walkthroughs and live demos are minimum. Beta users are even better.

Signal 02

Early Adopters

Real users — even 10 or 100 — who are actively using the product and providing feedback. Testimonials and usage data matter.

Signal 03

Repeatable Process

You can acquire users consistently — not just once. Investors want to see a playbook, not a lucky break.

Signal 04

Willingness to Pay

Even a single paying customer (at R50/month for Lesedi Edge) proves the market will pay. Free signups alone are weaker evidence.

Funding Stages

Pre-Seed through Series C — what each stage means and where UmkhoAI sits

Startup fundraising follows a progression of rounds. Each round has a typical size, typical investor type, and specific milestones expected before and after. Understanding this ladder is essential for planning your fundraising strategy.

Stage 0 · Bootstrapped
Pre-Revenue / Self-Funded
Founders use personal funds, friends and family. No external investors. Focus: build the MVP and prove basic viability. UmkhoAI is currently building in this zone — a strength, not a weakness, as it shows lean execution.
Stage 1 · Pre-Seed
R500k – R5M · Angel Investors / Accelerators (YC, Grindstone)
For companies with a working prototype and early user interest. Investors bet on the team and vision. Typical equity given: 10–20%. YC's standard deal is $500k for 7% (Post-Money SAFE). UmkhoAI's target zone: NOW – mid-2026.
Stage 2 · Seed
R5M – R30M · Seed VCs, Angel Syndicates
Product is live, users are growing, early revenue exists. Investors want to see MRR, user growth, and a clear path to Series A. Typical equity: 15–25%. UmkhoAI target: Jan 2027 – Jan 2028.
Stage 3 · Series A
R30M – R150M · Institutional VCs
Strong product-market fit, consistent MRR growth, proven unit economics. Investors fund scaling of sales, marketing, and engineering. Typical equity: 20–30%. UmkhoAI target: 2028–2029.
Stage 4 · Series B / C
R150M+ · Growth Equity, Late-Stage VCs
Proven revenue engine, expanding to new markets or product lines. Series B funds growth; Series C prepares for IPO or major acquisition. UmkhoAI target: 2030+.

Credibility Signals

MOUs, LOIs, associations, memberships — the documents that build investor trust

Beyond the product itself, investors need to trust the team, the partnerships, and the market traction. Formal documents and institutional affiliations are credibility signals — proof that serious organisations take you seriously.

Letter of Intent (LOI)

A non-binding document from a potential customer or partner indicating their serious interest in doing business with you. It signals intent to purchase or partner — not a contract yet, but much stronger than a verbal conversation.

UmkhoAI receiving an LOI from a municipality to pilot Lesedi Gov Pro is a powerful investor signal — it shows the government tier is actively considering the product before a contract is signed.
Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)

A formal but non-binding agreement between two parties outlining the terms of a planned collaboration. Stronger than an LOI — both parties have agreed on the scope, timeline, and intent. Common in government and enterprise contexts.

UmkhoAI signing an MOU with a provincial education department to deploy Lesedi Enterprise Nano across 50 schools is proof of institutional partnership traction — exactly what Series A investors want to see at scale.
Strategic Partnership Agreement

A binding agreement where another company commits to co-selling, co-developing, or distributing your product. Much stronger than an MOU — revenue implications are clear.

A distribution partnership with a major SA telecoms company to bundle Lesedi Edge V.1 with device purchases — reaching millions of users instantly.

Key Financial Metrics

ARR, MRR, Burn Rate, Churn Rate — the numbers that tell investors how healthy your company is

These are the metrics every investor reviews in your pitch deck and data room. As Finance Officer, you must be able to calculate, explain, and improve each of these.

MRR — Monthly Recurring Revenue

The predictable revenue a business generates each month from subscriptions or recurring contracts. MRR is the heartbeat of a SaaS company.

If UmkhoAI has 2,000 Lesedi Edge subscribers at R50/month plus 3 Enterprise Nano clients at R80,000/month: MRR = (2,000 × R50) + (3 × R80,000) = R100,000 + R240,000 = R340,000 MRR.
ARR — Annual Recurring Revenue

ARR = MRR multiplied by 12. This annualises your monthly revenue to give investors a yearly view. It is not total revenue — it is the run-rate of recurring revenue only.

If MRR = R340,000, then ARR = R340,000 × 12 = R4,080,000. UmkhoAI would report "R4.08M ARR" to investors.
Burn Rate

The speed at which your company spends cash each month. Gross Burn = total monthly spend. Net Burn = monthly spend minus monthly revenue. Lower is better — it means more runway.

If UmkhoAI spends R500,000/month and earns R340,000 MRR: Net Burn = R500,000 − R340,000 = R160,000/month.
Runway

How many months of operation remain at the current burn rate before cash runs out. Runway = Cash in Bank divided by Net Burn Rate. Minimum safe runway for fundraising: 12–18 months.

If UmkhoAI has R2M in the bank and net burn = R160,000/month: Runway = R2,000,000 ÷ R160,000 = 12.5 months. Start fundraising now.
Churn Rate

The percentage of customers who cancel their subscription in a given month. Lower is better. SaaS companies should target less than 2% monthly churn. High churn signals product-market fit problems.

If UmkhoAI starts June with 2,000 Edge subscribers and 60 cancel: Monthly churn = 60 / 2,000 = 3%. This is above benchmark — investigate why users are leaving.
Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

Measures whether existing customers spend more over time (expansion exceeds churn). NRR above 100% means net positive. This is the metric that makes enterprise SaaS investors excited.

Enterprise Nano clients starting at R100,000/month and expanding to R130,000/month while none cancel = NRR of 130%. This is exceptional and signals deep customer value.

Market Sizing

TAM, SAM, SOM — and why 600 million offline Africans is UmkhoAI's most powerful slide

Investors need to know: how big is the market opportunity? Undersell it and they will not invest. Overstate it without credible data and they will not trust you. The TAM/SAM/SOM framework is the industry standard for presenting market size credibly.

TAM — Total Addressable Market

The maximum possible revenue if your product captured 100% of the global or total market. This is the ceiling — rarely achievable, but used to show the scale of the opportunity.

TAM for UmkhoAI: approximately 1.4 billion people in Sub-Saharan Africa (World Bank, 2024). If just the offline AI productivity market at $5/user/year, the TAM is $7 billion.
SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market

The portion of the TAM your product can realistically target given your current technology, geography, and go-to-market strategy.

SAM for UmkhoAI: approximately 60M South Africans + 20M Zimbabweans + 15M Zambians = approximately 95M people in SA-adjacent markets where Lesedi's 12 SA languages are relevant. At R50/year, SAM is approximately R4.75B.
SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market

The realistic share of the SAM you can capture in the next 3–5 years given your team, budget, and strategy. This is the number investors actually hold you to.

SOM for UmkhoAI: Target 0.5% of the South African offline population. 600M × ~10% SA share = 60M → 0.5% = 300,000 subscribers. 300,000 × R600/year = R180M ARR by Year 5.

The 600M Opportunity

According to the GSMA Mobile Economy Sub-Saharan Africa 2023 report, approximately 600 million people in Sub-Saharan Africa remain offline — representing the world's largest unconnected population. Data costs in SA average R149/GB compared to R1/GB in the US (ICASA 2023).

This creates UmkhoAI's core market thesis: AI access is currently blocked by data costs and connectivity infrastructure. An offline-first AI removes both barriers simultaneously.

Customer-Driven Raising

Using customers — not just VCs — as your primary funding source strategy

The best fundraising strategy is one where your customers fund your growth. Customer-driven raising means designing your revenue model so customer payments provide working capital — reducing VC dependency and improving your negotiating leverage.

Strategy 01

Annual Pre-Pay Incentives

Offer enterprise clients 10–15% discount for paying 12 months upfront. A R600,000/year contract paid on day 1 equals R600k cash in hand vs R50k/month. This funds hiring and operations immediately.

CASH FLOW STRATEGY
Strategy 02

Enterprise Pilots as Funded R&D

Charge enterprises R50,000–R200,000 to co-develop custom Lesedi Nano models. They get a bespoke AI — you get funded development. This is non-dilutive capital.

NON-DILUTIVE
Strategy 03

Government Grants and Contracts

SEDA, DTI, IDC, GSMA Innovation Fund — government bodies actively fund AI projects serving underserved populations. These are non-dilutive and validate your social impact mission.

GRANT FUNDING
Strategy 04

Revenue-Based Financing (RBF)

Lenders provide capital in exchange for a fixed percentage of monthly revenue until a cap is repaid. No equity given. Works well for SaaS with predictable MRR. Available from Futuregrowth, IFC, and specialist RBF providers.

UmkhoAI Enterprise Nano: Customer Funding Model

The Gov Pro product (R5M–R35M contracts) is even more powerful: a single signed government contract at R5M could fund UmkhoAI's operations for over 2 years at current burn rates.

UmkhoAI Profit Margin Analysis

Gross Margin

Revenue minus the direct cost of delivering the service (Cost of Goods Sold). For SaaS, COGS includes hosting, support, and model inference costs. UmkhoAI's offline model means no cloud inference costs — a structural cost advantage.

Lesedi Edge: R50/month price. Direct cost: approximately R8/month (app store fees plus customer support allocation). Gross margin = (R50 − R8) / R50 = 84%. This is above typical SaaS (70–80%) and aligns with UmkhoAI's reported 71–82% margins.
Unit Economics

The revenue and cost associated with a single customer. Healthy unit economics mean each additional customer makes the business more profitable. Key ratio: LTV (Lifetime Value) to CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost). LTV/CAC greater than 3:1 is the investor benchmark.

Lesedi Edge subscriber: LTV = R50 × 24 months avg = R1,200. If CAC = R80 (digital marketing cost), LTV/CAC = 15:1. This is exceptional — well above the 3:1 benchmark.
